ClearWorth brings together things I’ve spent more than ten years learning about: working with data, solving practical problems, and getting a better handle on my finances. Building the app has given me a way to put that experience into something other people can use.

What my work taught me

My professional background is in enterprise GIS, location intelligence, databases, and automation. Working with ArcGIS Enterprise, SQL Server, and data workflows has taught me to look closely at how information moves through a system and whether it helps the person using it.

A workflow can make perfect sense to the person who built it and still be confusing to someone else. I try to keep that in mind with ClearWorth. The inputs need to make sense, the results need to be checked, and the instructions need to help someone use the app without having to guess.

The things outside work matter, too

I enjoy finding ways to use technology to solve everyday problems. Sometimes that means organizing information. Other times it means taking a task that’s frustrating or repetitive and finding a better way to do it.

Family and years of coaching youth sports have shaped how I approach that work. Preparation and communication matter. So do accountability and sticking with something when it gets difficult. I have a reminder for myself: “Out work, out learn, out last!”

Getting a clearer picture of our finances

Personal and financial growth are things I’m still working on. I want to understand where money goes, how expenses fit around income, and what debt and savings mean for the goals ahead.

Those interests led to some of the questions behind ClearWorth. What’s due next? Which expenses need to come out of this paycheck? What does the rest of the month look like? How do those decisions affect what we’re trying to save or pay off?

Having the information in one place makes those questions easier to work through. It also makes it easier to come back to the plan regularly, instead of only looking at it when something needs attention.

Building something other people can use

I wanted to do more with what I’d learned. The projects that interest me usually start with a problem I recognize: information that’s hard to follow, a repeated task that takes too long, or a decision that would be easier with a clearer picture.

ClearWorth grew out of my interest in financial planning and the skills I’d developed through my work. I’m building it to help people organize their finances and understand their options. That’s also what I hope to do with the other projects I share here.

What I keep coming back to

  • Start with the real workflow. Build around the income, expenses, dates, and goals people actually need to manage.
  • Make the information understandable. Weekly, monthly, and annual views should help answer different questions about the same finances.
  • Take care of the data. Validation, backups, access controls, and useful documentation are part of the work.
  • Use the app and listen to feedback. Testing shows me where a layout is awkward, an import is confusing, or an instruction needs to be clearer.
  • Keep learning. There’s usually something I can understand better or improve.

Where ClearWorth goes from here

Over time, my technical work and my interest in finances started to fit together. Experience with data and automation gave me a way to build the tools I wanted to use.

ClearWorth brings income and expenses, financial calendars, savings and debt goals, projections, and charts into a local-first Windows application. I’m continuing to test and refine it, paying attention to the calculations as well as how it feels to use.

I’ll use this blog to share that progress, including what works, what needs another look, and what I learn along the way.
