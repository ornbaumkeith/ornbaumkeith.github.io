Understanding what comes in and what goes out is one of the most important habits my wife and I have developed around our finances. It isn't just about knowing whether we have enough to cover this month's expenses. It's about understanding where our money is going, preparing for what's ahead, and working together toward the goals we've set.

Why knowing your numbers matters

It's easy to know how much money you make and still feel uncertain about what is available after bills, everyday spending, and other commitments. When income and expenses are written down together, the numbers become a conversation rather than a guess. We can spot recurring costs, see which payments are approaching, and make decisions with a better picture of our finances.

Just as important, keeping a history of previous months gives us a reference point. Rather than relying on memory, we can look at what actually happened, discuss what changed, and use that information to plan for upcoming months.

A spreadsheet that grew with us

I originally created the Personal Finance Planner for our own household. It has gone through more than ten revisions, with added functionality in every version. The changes came from personal experience: finding an easier way to organize our information, understand our expenses, and learn what worked for us.

The workbook brings income, expenses, financial summaries, assets, debts, and accounts together in one place. Its monthly metrics update as entries change, and the dashboard updates automatically to help show the broader financial picture. Drop-down menus make recording and updating income and expenses more straightforward. These features exist because I found them helpful in making the process easier to follow, not because I wanted to make the spreadsheet complicated.

How we use it together

My wife and I now sit down every two weeks for a finance meeting. We review the spreadsheet together, talk about income and expenses, and look ahead. Those conversations cover our financial goals for the year, upcoming bills and expenses, planned events, and anything else that could affect our budget.

Sometimes we're simply checking that things are on track. Other times we're discussing a change in priorities or how an upcoming event fits into our plan. Having the same information in front of both of us makes those conversations easier. The spreadsheet has given us better visibility into our financial health and a tool that we can both follow and understand.

What you'll find in the planner

The workbook includes monthly worksheets to record activity and review automatically updating financial metrics, an updating dashboard for a broader overview, and drop-down menus to help manage expense and income entries. Dedicated views for assets, debts, and accounts keep related information together. Looking back through recorded months can help with future planning.

This is a standalone Excel resource. The lessons I learned from it helped inspire ClearWorth, a separate application I hope will eventually make financial conversations and planning even easier. The spreadsheet is not part of that app.

I'd love to hear what you think

After more than ten revisions, I still see room for improvement. If you download the planner and find a feature useful—or discover something that could work better—I'd welcome your feedback. Ideas from people who use the tool can help shape its next improvements.
