Why I Built Pairvoy for My Wife and Me

I built Pairvoy to help my wife and me stick with our own goals, build better habits, and cheer each other on.

Keith OrnbaumNov 23, 20262 min read

In this article

  • Big goals come down to ordinary days
  • Let your people join the fun

My wife and I have goals we want to reach together, and we each have goals of our own. I wanted a way for us to support each other in both. If I’m working toward something that matters to me, it helps to know she’s in my corner. I want her to feel the same way about the things she’s working toward, even when they’re entirely her own.

That’s where the idea for Pairvoy started. I built it to help us follow through on the things we say are important and to make it a little more fun along the way.

Big goals come down to ordinary days

Setting a goal feels good. The harder part is figuring out what to do about it on a Tuesday when work ran long and dinner still needs to be made.

Say your goal is to get in better shape. That’s easy enough to write down. “Walk for 20 minutes after dinner” gives you something you can actually do today. Make that walk a regular part of your week and you’ve built a habit you can build on. The bigger result takes time, but you can see the work adding up long before you get there.

I wanted Pairvoy to make that part easier. You can break a goal into steps, put actions on the calendar, and open My checklist to see what’s on your plate today. You can also look back at your progress, which comes in handy on the weeks when it feels like you haven’t done much.

Let your people join the fun

One thing I’ve learned is how much it helps to have my wife and family behind me. Sometimes support is as simple as someone remembering what you’re working toward and asking how it went. Other times, they might want to join you. A walk after dinner can start as your goal and turn into something the family looks forward to.

In Pairvoy, you can work on personal goals alongside shared ones and choose what progress to share. I added XP, levels, and achievements because checking something off should feel good. It gives you another reason to celebrate the effort that usually goes unnoticed, especially when the finish line is still a ways off.

I made Pairvoy for my wife and me, and I’m sharing it in the hope that it helps other couples, too. If there’s something you’ve both been talking about doing, or something one of you could use a little encouragement on, start there. Pick one thing you can do this week and see how it feels to have someone cheering you on.
