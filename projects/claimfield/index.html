From map planning to field records

One project, from boundary to field package.

ClaimField helps organize the work around planning and staking mining claims. Define areas of interest, generate and edit claim geometry, review terrain and reference layers, and prepare the points and routes a field crew needs.

The project connects spatial planning with the next steps: assigning people, scheduling visits, preparing maps and notices, and recording actual field observations. Planned dates and geometry stay distinct from actual staking facts.

A connected workflow

Prepare. Review. Schedule. Record.

  1. Define the project

    Draw or import one or more areas of interest, choose the claim type and generate planning geometry.

  2. Review the ground

    Adjust claims, inspect reference layers and terrain, and identify tie points. Address warnings and verify the controlling records.

  3. Coordinate the field

    Set route endpoints and workers, generate terrain-aware routes, and edit the planned visits in Scheduling.

  4. Prepare & bring back records

    Export maps, notices and worker checklists. Import reviewed actual observations and dates to update field records and supporting documents.

Within the workspace

Spatial detail, connected to practical tasks.

Claim planning & editing

Generate lode, placer and mill-site planning geometry, work with multiple AOIs, and edit claim edges, positions and orientation. Geometry checks and review flags help identify issues.

Terrain & elevation

Inspect AOI elevation statistics and colored grids in 3D, overhead and side views, using Terrain3D elevation tiles. Review elevation profiles and export sampled data.

Terrain-aware crew routing

Plan routes for multiple workers, set access/start/finish points and consider sampled slope and travel cost. Review missing terrain, steep sections and real access conditions.

Calendar, Timeline & List

Schedule crews or individual workers, move and resize visits, review absence and assignment conflicts, and export project or monthly .ics calendar files.

Measure, mark up & tie in

Use measurement and COGO tools, add editable points, lines, polygons, arrows and labels, and manage tie-point information alongside map context.

Maps & document preparation

Prepare full-page NOLs, COLs, project summaries and separate map sheets. Choose field maps with or without routes and monochrome filing-map layouts with PLSS context.

Field checklists & attachments

Export per-worker CSV templates and review imports of actual staking dates, names, notes and coordinates. Store project-related maps, PDFs and GIS files as attachments.

GIS handoff

Export spatial data and QGIS/QField preparation packages. Check layer forms, CRS, basemaps and device accuracy in the receiving GIS workflow before field use.

Completion & filing review

Review missing facts, spatial inconsistencies and filing countdowns based on entered location dates and supported state rules. Local recording requirements still need verification.

Architecture & technical details

A GIS interface with an account-scoped backend.

These details describe the reviewed 0.27.5 application source. Public-beta hosting and account access are not yet announced.

01 / Interface

Browser workspace

React and TypeScript coordinate project state, map tools, document forms and the shared scheduling views.

02 / Processing

Geometry & terrain

Map rendering, geodesic calculations and background workers support interactive planning and route analysis.

03 / Persistence

Private account API

A Node.js service saves account-owned projects, schedule records and project attachments.

Implemented technologies and their roles
LayerImplementation
Interface & buildReact, TypeScript and Vite; shared project models and modular feature panes.
Map renderingMapLibre GL with a Leaflet fallback; GeoJSON planning geometry and reference layers.
Spatial calculationsTurf and GeographicLib for geometry operations and geodesic measurements.
Reference & elevation dataArcGIS REST services, BLM PLSS and land-reference sources, USGS map services, and WorldElevation3D/Terrain3D. Source availability and resolution vary.
Background processingWeb Workers for terrain routing; bounded raster requests, caching and cancellation help keep map interaction responsive.
SchedulingOne account schedule ledger feeds Calendar, Timeline and List. Generated route visits remain editable; computer-local display time is used.
Backend & storageNode.js HTTP API with private filesystem JSON records and attachment blobs. The current backend is designed for one server process.
Documents & exchangejsPDF for PDFs, JSZip for packages, plus HTML, CSV, .ics and spatial exports.

Account ownership

Server requests check account/project ownership. Sign-in uses scrypt password hashes and server sessions with HttpOnly, SameSite cookies. Browser drafts are scoped by account; exported files and shared device profiles need their own protection.

Performance & persistence

Memoized geometry and virtualized timeline rows/columns limit repeated work. Local drafts and server-saved projects are distinct. Online services are required for live reference data; the app is not presented as fully offline or certified at 60 fps.

A planning aid, with visible limits

Support the review. Keep the facts traceable.

Documents follow entered facts

NOLs, COLs and maps are preparation drafts. Missing information is flagged. Proposed field points and estimated route dates do not establish discovery, actual staking, valid location or legal filing.

Verify the land and the ground

Reference layers and terrain samples do not certify mineral-entry availability, legal access, recovered survey monuments or safe passage. Verify current agency records, state and county requirements, and actual field conditions.
